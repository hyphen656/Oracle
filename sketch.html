The story

Two machines in a corridor at Bell Labs

In 1953 Claude Shannon built a machine out of relays that tried to guess whether you would say “right” or “left.” It was a simplified copy of one his colleague David Hagelbarger had built first, and it kept score by knocking a steel ball down a row of fifty others. Shannon knew exactly how beatable it was, and said so:

A mathematical analysis of the strategy used in this machine shows that it can be beaten by the best possible play in the ratio 3:1. […] It is extremely difficult to carry out this program mentally because of the amount of memory and calculation necessary. Shannon, A Mind-Reading (?) Machine, 1953

That is the whole subject of this page, stated seventy years early. The machine is not unbeatable. You simply cannot beat it in your head.
